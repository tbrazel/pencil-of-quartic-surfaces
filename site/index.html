Lines in a quartic pencil

Members Xλ = {f − λQ = 0} of a real pencil of quartic surfaces in ℝℙ³, shown in the chart w = 1. Of the 320 complex lines in the pencil, 12 are real. Each one lies on a single member. Stop there, cut with a plane through the line, and watch the three nodes of the plane section add up to the local index, marked at Q.

How to read it. In the plane H, the section H ∩ Xλ is the line L together with a cubic curve (dashed). The cubic meets L in three points, the nodes of the section; they are where H is tangent to the surface. At a node p, Q(p)/F⊥(p) is how far the surface moves when λ changes (the displacement), and FH′(p) is how fast the tangent plane turns away from H along L (the rotation). Rotating H moves the nodes and changes every individual term, but the sum stays fixed: it is the obstruction to deforming L to nearby members, and its sign is the local index over ℝ.

The pencil. f is a random Kostlan quartic and Q = (x² + y² + z² + w²)² + 0.12·g for another random quartic g. Q is positive on ℝℙ³, so the real members are the level sets of f/Q, and λ runs over [min f/Q, max f/Q]. All 320 lines were found by homotopy continuation. The picture shows the part of the chart inside a ball of radius 3.3.

Conventions. Signs use the Kass–Wickelgren chart S = span(e₀ + a e₂ + b e₃, e₁ + c e₂ + d e₃), the monomial basis u⁴, …, r⁴, and the determinant det[Q|L, uF₁, uF₂, rF₁, rF₂]. They agree across random changes of basis and between charts. A different overall convention would flip every sign at once.
